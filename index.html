Background

From the mangroves of
São Luís to global science

Born and raised on the Ilha de Upaon-Açu in the heart of the Amazon, I developed an early fascination with the ecological complexity of tropical ecosystems — particularly the mangroves near my childhood home.

That curiosity drove me from Environmental Engineering in Maranhão to the Brazilian National Institute for Space Research (INPE), where I completed my M.Sc. and Ph.D. in Remote Sensing, and then to a postdoctoral fellowship at UCLA and NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory.

Today, my research focuses on the intersection of remote sensing technology, carbon biogeochemistry, and climate policy — producing work that has appeared in Nature, Science, Nature Climate Change, and more than 70 peer-reviewed articles.

I am also a member of the Scientific Panel for the Amazon (SPA/SDSN) and have contributed to the 5th National Greenhouse Gas Inventory of Brazil.

Research Areas

Investigating the carbon–climate–
land use nexus

🌿

Forest Carbon Dynamics

Quantifying above- and below-ground biomass stocks, carbon losses from deforestation and fragmentation, and recovery rates in secondary forests across Brazilian biomes using LiDAR, hyperspectral, and optical data.

🔥

Fire Ecology & Emissions

Mapping burned areas, analyzing fire regimes across Amazonia, Cerrado and Caatinga, and estimating CO₂, CH₄ and N₂O emissions — including near-real-time detection systems for land managers and government agencies.

🛰️

Remote Sensing & ML

Developing deep-learning pipelines (Planet NICFI, GEDI, Sentinel) for forest degradation mapping, secondary vegetation age mapping, and multi-sensor data fusion for ecosystem monitoring at continental scales.

🌍

Climate Policy & REDD+

Assessing Brazil's compliance with its Forest Reference Emission Levels, analyzing the effectiveness of protected areas, and providing scientific evidence for international climate negotiations including COP commitments.

🪴

Secondary Forest Regeneration

Modeling growth trajectories, carbon debt accumulation, and climate benefits of secondary forests — with projections to 2100 under fire legacy and no-fire scenarios using Chapman-Richards growth modeling.

🌊

Coastal & Wetland Ecosystems

Investigating mangrove carbon stocks, sea-level rise vulnerability, and flooded forest dynamics in Eastern Amazonia, including post-fire carbon emission pipelines for Amazonian igapó and várzea forests.

Current Projects

Leading science with
real-world impact

Active · 2023–present

YBYRÁ-BR: Spatio-temporal Quantification of CO₂ Emissions and Removals by Brazilian Forests

Ambitious national-scale effort integrating optical, radar and LiDAR satellite data with AI to separate gross emissions (deforestation, fire, degradation) from removals (secondary forest growth), feeding into Brazil's Paris Agreement reporting.

CNPq/MCTI · Principal Investigator
Active · 2024–present

Amazonian BioTechQuilombo: Biodiversity Monitoring in Quilombola Territories

Pioneering initiative combining advanced remote sensing with participatory science, mobile apps, and traditional ecological knowledge in quilombola communities to support autonomous territorial monitoring and biodiversity conservation.

Amazônia +10 · Principal Investigator · R$ 12.6M
Active · 2024–present

Climate-Resilient Forest Restoration in the Eastern Amazon

International collaboration led by Lancaster University focused on large-scale tropical forest restoration in Pará, maximizing co-benefits for climate, biodiversity and local communities through advanced spatial modelling.

University of Lancaster (UK) · Collaborator
Active · 2024–present

Resilience of Biodiversity in the Gurupi Biological Reserve (PELD Phase II)

Long-term ecological research program investigating natural regeneration trajectories after forest disturbance, contributing to understanding the resilience of Amazonian ecosystems under combined pressures of fire and land use change.

CNPq PELD Network · Collaborator
